1 Introduction
Etesia brings systematic, institutional-grade asset management to Stellar by integrating the network’s
core DeFi and wallet building blocks. We are building two things: new products that supply genuinely
diversifying return streams, and a free tool that lets users allocate across them. Etesia already runs a live
systematic vault on Hyperliquid; this project ports and extends that infrastructure to Stellar.
Real diversiﬁcation needs return streams that are not correlated, and on Stellar today the investable universe
is almost entirely directional crypto beta: most assets move with Bitcoin. The new products supply what
is missing. They are systematic trend-following vaults, which carry a structurally low and often negative
correlation to buy-and-hold crypto (the live strategy runs near −0.22 correlation to Bitcoin). The vaults are
the diversifying building blocks; the Portfolio Builder is the tool that allocates across them. This document
describes the technical architecture and the Stellar protocol integrations covered by this submission:
1. Long-only Trend-Following Vault, on Soroswap: a spot trend-following vault over Stellar assets, long
while an asset trends up and rotated into stablecoins otherwise. All execution runs through Soroswap;
stablecoins held during ﬂat phases earn yield in Blend v2 lending pools. It needs only spot DEX liquidity,
no perpetuals, so it ships ﬁrst.
2. Long-Short Systematic Vault: the full long and short CTA, ported from the strategy Etesia runs live on
Hyperliquid and built behind avenue-agnostic execution interface. One trend-following strategy drives two
interchangeable execution paths: native perpetuals via whichever Stellar perp venue reaches mainnet
ﬁrst (win-trader, formerly stellars.ﬁnance, is the primary candidate; Zenex and Noether are monitored
as alternatives), or synthetic short exposure constructed atomically via Blend v2 and Soroswap. Signal
generation and execution run from our oﬀ-chain Python infrastructure; the venue is a thin adapter, not
a dependency, so the vault does not hinge on any single third-party venue going live.

3. Portfolio Builder, a tool and not a vault: a non-custodial allocation tool that lets users construct a
diversiﬁed, risk-balanced portfolio across Etesia vaults and other Stellar assets, using the risk parity
(Equal Risk Contribution) allocation engine. Custody stays in the user’s wallet and in the underlying
vaults; the tool only computes and routes allocations.
All three share the same architectural principle: oﬀ-chain intelligence, on-chain (or venue-side) custody
and settlement. Quantitative computation (covariance estimation, allocation, signal generation, execution
scheduling) is too heavy for on-chain execution and runs on our Python infrastructure, the same stack that
operates our live Hyperliquid vault. Funds never touch our servers: custody sits in Soroban contracts, in
the underlying vaults, or in the venue’s vault primitive, and the oﬀ-chain engine can only propose actions
that pass hard-coded on-chain constraints. A compromised backend cannot move funds; the worst case is
a missed rebalance.

sleeves of the asset universe, and the venue on which each sleeve executes.

3.1 Integration detail: Soroswap
Soroswap’s routing API, which aggregates all Stellar liquidity (Soroban and classic), is integrated at two
levels.
• TWAP pricing. Time-weighted average prices computed from allowlisted Soroswap pools serve as
the on-chain reference for NAV computation and execution bounds. The vault contract reads TWAP
directly; the executor never supplies prices. TWAP sets min_amount_out on every rebalance leg.
• Swap execution. Vault rebalancing routes its swap legs through Soroswap’s aggregated liquidity.
Each leg speciﬁes {adapter, asset_in, asset_out, amount_in, min_amount_out}; the Soroswap adapter
translates this into calls against the Soroswap router contract. Slippage bounds are TWAP-derived and
enforced on-chain, so a manipulated pool causes the swap to fail rather than ﬁll badly.

3.2 Integration detail: Blend v2
Blend v2 lending pools are the yield destination for stablecoins held while the long-only vault is in a ﬂat
phase. Instead of parking idle USDC or EURC, the orchestrator routes it into Blend.
• Deposit ﬂow. When the strategy rotates an asset to stablecoins, the orchestrator deposits the surplus
into the Blend pool via cross-contract call.
• Withdraw ﬂow. When the strategy re-enters and the risky sleeve scales up, the orchestrator withdraws
from Blend, then executes the rebalance plan.
• Yield accrual. Blend lending yield accrues to the vault’s total managed assets, increasing NAV and
share price for all depositors.
// Idle stablecoins -> Blend v2 supply. The resulting position is read back by
// nav() via blend::deposited_value(), so accrued yield lifts share price directly.
fn park_in_blend(e: &Env, amount: i128) {

3.3 Integration detail: DeFindex
Each Etesia vault exposes the DeFindex Strategy interface, making it pluggable into any DeFindex vault
deployed through the DeFindex Factory:
• invest(amount) deposits into the strategy;
• unwind(amount) withdraws proportionally;
• harvest() compounds Blend yield and rebalancing gains.

3.4 Integration detail: Stellar Wallets Kit
Frontend wallet connectivity uses Stellar Wallets Kit (@creit-tech/stellar-wallets-kit), supporting Freighter,

4 Architecture Constraints
• Non-custodial by construction. Users deposit into contracts, not accounts we control. Withdrawals
are permissionless and never depend on our backend being online.
• Operator as proposer, not owner. The oﬀ-chain engine proposes rebalances. The vault independently
veriﬁes every proposal against on-chain invariants (asset allowlists, slippage bounds, notional caps,
cooldowns, pause state) and rejects anything outside them. A compromised backend cannot drain
funds; the worst case is a missed or suboptimal rebalance.
• Soroban compute limits. Covariance estimation and ERC optimization involve matrix operations
unsuited to on-chain execution. They run oﬀ-chain; only veriﬁcation logic, accounting and transfers
run on-chain.
• Transparency. Target weights are published on-chain before execution, and the allocation methodology
is documented, so any third party can verify that executed trades match the published policy.
• Venue-agnostic execution (long-short vault). The long-short strategy is independent of its execution
path: the signal engine, risk controls and position sizing are identical whether it trades a native perp
venue (win-trader primary; Zenex, Noether alternatives) or synthetic shorts built from Blend v2 and
Soroswap. The venue is a thin adapter behind an internal interface, not a dependency, so if no perp
venue reaches mainnet the synthetic path delivers the same exposure without touching strategy code.

6 Allocation Engine (Risk Parity)
The allocation engine is the risk parity (Equal Risk Contribution) engine that powers the Portfolio Builder
tool. Given a set of Etesia vaults and other Stellar assets, it computes weights so that each component
contributes equally to total portfolio risk. The tool routes these allocations to the user’s wallet and the
underlying vaults; it never takes custody.

deviates from target by more than a threshold (e.g., 10% relative). Both paths produce the same artifact:
a target weight vector published on-chain, followed by a rebalance plan whose legs are constructed to
minimize turnover (netting buys against sells) and respect per-leg slippage bounds.
7 Trend-Following Engine
The trend-following engine generates the target positions that drive the vaults, porting the systematic
model Etesia runs live on Hyperliquid. The signal aggregates multiple proprietary oscillators into a single
directional target per asset; their construction is proprietary and not detailed here. What matters for this
architecture is that output, a directional target per asset, and how it maps to each vault mandate:
• Long-only vault (Vault 1).The engine targetslong an asset while it trends up andﬂat otherwise, with the
ﬂat allocation rotated into stablecoins that earn yield in Blend v2. This vault trades spot on Soroswap
and needs no perpetuals, so it ships ﬁrst.
• Long-short vault (Vault 2).The engine targetslong in an uptrend andshort in a downtrend, the full CTA,
executed through a venue-agnostic interface: native perpetuals (win-trader primary; Zenex, Noether
alternatives) or synthetic shorts built from Blend v2 and Soroswap. The strategy output is identical
across paths.
Position sizes are scaled to a portfolio volatility target and bounded by the liquidity limits described later.
Signal parameters are calibrated by walk-forward validation, never in-sample. The live strategy’s realized
correlation to Bitcoin is near−0.22, which is what makes it a genuine diversiﬁer for an otherwise directional
Stellar universe.

8 On-Chain Design (Soroban)
On Soroban the vault contract is the trust anchor, and the oﬀ-chain engine is only a proposer. Every
protection that matters lives in the Rust contract, so it can refuse any unsafe action on its own even if
the backend is fully compromised. Soroban is explicit about four things the contract has to get right,
and we document each below: storage (typed, tiered, with explicit TTLs), authorization (per-entry-point
require_auth), events (the on-chain audit trail), and invariants (the checks that gate every state change).
The guiding rule is that the contract is self-suﬃcient: a valid signature from the executor key is necessary
but never suﬃcient, since a proposal also has to pass every invariant below.
8.1 Contract storage layout
Soroban storage is typed and split into tiers with explicit time-to-live (TTL): instance storage for small
contract-wide conﬁg (read cheaply on every call, extended with the contract), and persistent storage for
per-account balances and allowlist membership. Keys are a single contracttype enum, so every access is
type-checked at compile time.
#[contracttype]
pub enum DataKey {
Admin, Executor, Guardian, // role addresses (instance)
Paused, // bool kill-switch (instance)
RiskParams, // slippage bps, caps, cooldown (instance)
Nonce, // monotonic rebalance counter (instance)
LastRebalance, // ledger timestamp of last exec (instance)
TotalShares, // S, total SEP-41 share supply (instance)
Assets, // Vec<Address>, capped NAV universe (instance)
TargetEpoch, // current published-weights epoch (instance)
Balance(Address), // s_i, shares held by an account (persistent)
AssetOk(Address), // asset allowlist membership (persistent)
AdapterOk(Address), // DEX-adapter allowlist membership (persistent)
}
// reads are tier-explicit; touched entries are TTL-bumped to avoid expiry
fn paused(e: &Env) -> bool {
e.storage().instance().get(&DataKey::Paused).unwrap_or(false)
}
fn shares_of(e: &Env, a: &Address) -> i128 {
e.storage().persistent().get(&DataKey::Balance(a.clone())).unwrap_or(0)
}
Allowlist membership is a keyed O(1) read on the hot path (AssetOk, AdapterOk); a separate admin-maintained,
length-capped Assets vector (the 10–30 asset universe) backs NAV enumeration, so the contract only ever
iterates a bounded, admin-set list, never unbounded user input.

// A = NAV. Priced on-chain via the pricing layer; the caller supplies nothing.
fn nav(e: &Env) -> i128 {
let vault = e.current_contract_address();
let mut total: i128 = 0;
for asset in assets(e).iter() { // bounded admin-set universe
let bal = token::Client::new(e, &asset).balance(&vault);
total += bal * pricing::twap(e, &asset) / PRICE_SCALE;
}
total + blend::deposited_value(e) // Blend supply accrues to NAV
}

pub fn deposit(e: Env, from: Address, assets_in: i128) -> i128 {
from.require_auth(); // depositor authorizes the pull
if paused(&e) { panic_with_error!(&e, Error::Paused); }
if assets_in <= 0 { panic_with_error!(&e, Error::ZeroAmount); }
let s = total_shares(&e); // S
let a = nav(&e); // A, priced before the inflow
let shares = if s == 0 { assets_in } // 1:1 bootstrap
else { assets_in * s / a }; // floor(a*S/A): rounds to vault
if shares <= 0 { panic_with_error!(&e, Error::ZeroShares); }
token::Client::new(&e, &underlying(&e))
.transfer(&from, &e.current_contract_address(), &assets_in);
mint_shares(&e, &from, shares); // S += shares; Balance(from) +=
e.events().publish((symbol_short!("deposit"), from), (assets_in, shares, a, s));
shares
}
// Pro-rata IN-KIND redemption: a proportional slice of EVERY vault asset.
// No oracle is consulted, so withdrawal never depends on price or backend liveness.
pub fn redeem(e: Env, to: Address, shares: i128) {
to.require_auth(); // burn only the caller 's shares
let s = total_shares(&e);
if shares <= 0 || shares > shares_of(&e, &to) {
panic_with_error!(&e, Error::BadShares);
}
let vault = e.current_contract_address();
for asset in assets(&e).iter() {
let bal = token::Client::new(&e, &asset).balance(&vault);
let out = bal * shares / s; // floor(s*b_k/S): rounds to vault
if out > 0 { token::Client::new(&e, &asset).transfer(&vault, &to, &out); }
}
burn_shares(&e, &to, shares); // S -= shares; Balance(to) -=
e.events().publish((symbol_short!("withdraw"), to), shares);
}
The exact-asset SEP-56 withdraw (burn ⌈aS/A⌉ shares for a requested asset amount) is also implemented,
but because it has to size the burn from NAV it follows the deposit availability rule: usable whenever NAV
can be priced. The pro-rata redeem path needs no price at all and is therefore the always-available exit.
8.3 Authorization model
Every entry point gates on require_auth for exactly one role. The executor key that proposes trades holds
no transfer authority, so theft of that key cannot move user funds, it can only attempt rebalances, which
still have to clear every invariant.
Role Authority (enforced via require_auth)
User Permissionless deposit, withdraw, redeem of own shares.
Executor May call execute_rebalance only; no transfer authority.
Guardian Pause / unpause; emergency de-risk into stablecoins.
Admin Updates allowlists and risk parameters under timelock.
fn require_role(e: &Env, key: DataKey) { // generic role gate
let who: Address = e.storage().instance().get(&key).unwrap();
who.require_auth();
}
// deposit / withdraw / redeem -> caller.require_auth() (any user, own funds)
// execute_rebalance -> require_role(e, DataKey::Executor)
// pause / emergency_derisk -> require_role(e, DataKey::Guardian)
// set_allowlist / set_params (timelocked) -> require_role(e, DataKey::Admin)
8.4 Events
Beyond the SEP-56 Deposit/Withdraw events, the vault publishes its target weights before execution and a
per-rebalance record after, so the full policy trail is reconstructable from the ledger alone.
// published before any trade: commits the engine to a target the ledger records

8.5 execute_rebalance: plan format and on-chain veriﬁcation
The executor submits a structured plan {nonce, deadline, target_weights_ref, legs[]}, where each leg is
{adapter, asset_in, asset_out, amount_in, min_amount_out}. The contract re-derives everything it can and
rejects the whole plan on any failed check, so an incoherent or malicious proposal reverts atomically rather
than partially ﬁlling. It enforces, and reverts on violation of, all of the following:
• nonce equals the expected value (replay protection) and current ledger time ≤ deadline;
• every adapter, asset_in and asset_out is on the admin-maintained allowlist;
• every leg carries min_amount_out at least as tight as the bound the contract recomputes from its own
TWAP and the maximum-slippage cap, so a manipulated pool or a self-dealing leg fails rather than ﬁlls
badly;
• aggregate notional is below a cap expressed as a fraction of NAV;
• a minimum cooldown has elapsed since the last rebalance, and the vault is not paused.
#[contracterror]
pub enum Error {
Paused = 1, NotAuthorized = 2, BadNonce = 3, Expired = 4, Cooldown = 5,
AssetNotAllowed = 6, AdapterNotAllowed = 7, Slippage = 8, NotionalCap = 9,
TwapUnavailable = 10, OracleStale = 11, OracleDivergence = 12, ZeroShares = 13,
}
pub fn execute_rebalance(e: Env, plan: Plan) {
require_role(&e, DataKey::Executor); // 1. authorized proposer only
if paused(&e) { panic_with_error!(&e, Error::Paused); }
let now = e.ledger().timestamp();
if plan.nonce != next_nonce(&e) { panic_with_error!(&e, Error::BadNonce); } // replay
if now > plan.deadline { panic_with_error!(&e, Error::Expired); } // freshness
if now < last_rebalance(&e) + cooldown(&e) { panic_with_error!(&e, Error::Cooldown); }
guard_pricing(&e); // 2. oracle sanity (next section)
let nav0 = nav(&e);
let mut notional: i128 = 0;
for leg in plan.legs.iter() { // 3. per-leg invariants
if !asset_ok(&e, &leg.asset_in) { panic_with_error!(&e, Error::AssetNotAllowed); }
if !asset_ok(&e, &leg.asset_out) { panic_with_error!(&e, Error::AssetNotAllowed); }
if !adapter_ok(&e, &leg.adapter) { panic_with_error!(&e, Error::AdapterNotAllowed); }
// contract recomputes the floor from TWAP; the proposer cannot widen it
let floor = twap_min_out(&e, &leg) * (BPS - max_slip_bps(&e)) / BPS;
if leg.min_amount_out < floor { panic_with_error!(&e, Error::Slippage); }
notional += value_in(&e, &leg);
}
if notional > nav0 * max_turnover_bps(&e) / BPS { // 4. aggregate notional cap
panic_with_error!(&e, Error::NotionalCap);
}
for leg in plan.legs.iter() { // 5. execute via allowlisted adapter
adapter::swap(&e, &leg); // honors the on-chain min_amount_out
}
bump_nonce(&e);
set_last_rebalance(&e, now); // 6. commit + emit
e.events().publish((symbol_short!("rebal"), executor(&e)),
(plan.nonce, plan.hash(&e), plan.legs.len() as u32));
}
Because min_amount_out is recomputed on-chain from the TWAP and a ﬁxed maximum-slippage parameter,
a backend that proposes an oﬀ-market or self-dealing leg simply fails the slippage check: it cannot widen
its own bound, and it never holds withdrawal rights to begin with.
8.6 Pricing layer and oracle trust model
NAV and execution bounds use a two-layer design, and the contract owns both reads. The executor never
supplies a price the contract trusts.
E T E S I A R E S E A R C H I N C· S Y S T E M A T I C T R A D I N G· T E C H N I C A L A R C H I T E C T U R E 11

• Primary: Soroswap TWAP. Time-weighted average prices from allowlisted Soroswap pools value NAV
and derive every min_amount_out. Using TWAP rather than spot means a single-block pool push cannot
move the bound.
• Circuit breaker: Reﬂector. The Reﬂector feed is compared against the TWAP; divergence beyond a
threshold, or staleness on either source, halts risk-increasing execution.
The failure modes are handled explicitly, and the design deliberately separates valuation liveness (needed to
price deposits) from execution liveness (needed to rebalance) from withdrawal, which depends on neither:
fn guard_pricing(e: &Env) {
let now = e.ledger().timestamp();
for asset in assets(e).iter() {
match pricing::soroswap_twap(e, &asset) { // Option<Quote>
None => panic_with_error!(e, Error::TwapUnavailable), // halt rebalancing
Some(q) if now - q.ts > MAX_AGE =>
panic_with_error!(e, Error::OracleStale),
Some(q) => match pricing::reflector(e, &asset) {
Some(r) if now - r.ts <= MAX_AGE => {
let dev = (q.price - r.price).abs() * BPS / r.price;
if dev > MAX_DEVIATION_BPS { // sources disagree
panic_with_error!(e, Error::OracleDivergence);
}
}
_ => panic_with_error!(e, Error::OracleStale), // breaker unavailable
},
}
}
}

10 Phase 2: Long-Short Systematic Vault
The long-short vault (Vault 2) is the full long and short CTA, porting Etesia’s live trend-following strategy
from Hyperliquid to Stellar and adding the short side that spot DEX execution cannot provide. It is built

• Native perpetuals. Directional exposure via whichever Stellar perp venue reaches mainnet ﬁrst. win-

• Synthetic shorts via Blend v2 + Soroswap. Short exposure built atomically on Stellar: deposit collateral

going live. Signal generation and execution run from our oﬀ-chain Python infrastructure, exactly as in
our Hyperliquid deployment. The hard engineering problem is not the strategy, which is proven and
running, but the execution infrastructure (clean market data, liquidity statistics, execution tooling) built
and calibrated to a thin, growing on-chain market.

10.4 Execution engine
The execution engine translates target positions into actions through a venue-agnostic interface (the same
abstraction our Hyperliquid engine uses), with two thin adapters behind it:
• Perp venue adapter. Order placement and amendment, slicing within participation limits, passive-ﬁrst
placement with re-quoting and escalation to marketable orders on urgency, ﬁll tracking, and continuous
reconciliation of internal state against venue-reported positions and balances.
• Synthetic-short adapter. Atomic Soroban transactions combining a Blend v2 collateral deposit and

target leverage, and unwinding via the reverse path with the same atomicity.

paths.
10.5 Signal engine and risk controls
The signal engine is the trend-following engine described above, here producing long/short target positions
per asset scaled to the portfolio volatility target. An independent risk daemon enforces drawdown guards,
leverage and position limits, data-staleness checks, and a kill switch that ﬂattens positions and halts trading;
it runs separately from the execution path so a strategy fault cannot disable risk enforcement.
On the synthetic-short path the risk daemon additionally monitors Blend v2 health factors and triggers
deleveraging before the liquidation threshold is reached. This replaces the funding-rate monitoring of the
perp path: same economic function (cost-of-carry and solvency management), diﬀerent metric.
10.6 Vault integration
Custody is path-dependent, and both options keep depositors non-custodial:
• Native perp path. Depositors access the strategy through the venue’s vault primitive: deposits, share
accounting, and proﬁt attribution are handled on Stellar, while our infrastructure trades the vault’s
account. Funds remain withdrawable through the venue independently of our infrastructure. This
mirrors the operating model of our live Hyperliquid vault.
• Synthetic-short path. Depositors use an Etesia SEP-56 Soroban vault, the same primitive as the
long-only vaults (see On-Chain Design). The orchestrator executes construction and unwinding on the
vault’s behalf under the same on-chain guardrails; funds are withdrawable permissionlessly through
the vault contract with no dependence on our backend being online.
10.7 Synthetic short construction
The synthetic short is a standard DeFi primitive, executed atomically on Stellar:
1. Deposit collateral (Asset A, e.g. USDC) into a Blend v2 pool.
2. Borrow the target Asset B against that collateral, subject to the pool’s LTV.

3. Swap B → A on Soroswap.
4. Re-deposit A for recursive collateralization, reaching eﬀective ∼2–3× exposure depending on LTV.
Steps 1–3 execute as a single atomic multi-invoke Soroban transaction; unwinding reverses the path with
the same atomicity. The carry cost is the Blend borrow rate on B minus the lending yield on A, comparable
to perp funding. The health factor is monitored continuously, and approaching the liquidation threshold
triggers an automatic partial unwind (via the risk daemon above). The tradable universe is limited to assets
with suﬃcient Blend liquidity and Soroswap depth, enforced through the per-asset participation caps of
the volume and liquidity model above.
