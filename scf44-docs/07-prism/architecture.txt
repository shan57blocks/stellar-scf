1. Introduction
High-Level Overview
Prism is a live multichain consumer DeFi hub: swaps, liquidity pools, perpetual futures, prediction markets,
yield, and portfolio management in a single interface, currently deployed on MegaETH and Arbitrum. Since
launching in March 2026, Prism has onboarded 6,000+ users in its first three months with zero paid
acquisition; over that period, monthly volume grew from $6.95M (March) to $33.3M (May) and TVL peaked at
$4M. Prism was selected into the Arbitrum Mentorship Program (13 teams from 900+ applicants).
This submission integrates Stellar as the settlement and issuance rail for Prism's RWA yield module. The
assets are Stellar-native: Etherfuse sovereign stablebonds issued on Stellar, held on Stellar accounts, settled
on Stellar. The friction is access: reaching these assets from an EVM wallet takes a multi-step bridge journey,
which in practice keeps EVM users out of the ecosystem. The venue is the right one: Stellar is the #4 network
for permissionless tokenized RWA value (RWA.xyz), and our first-market issuer's deepest liquidity sits there.
Prism's architecture closes the gap by making that venue feel EVM-native: one signature, no seed phrase, no
XLM. The award funds the deposit rail and the yield module to mainnet, adds an open-source Soroban vault
strategy on DeFindex (section 5.4), and routes an existing EVM user base onto Stellar rails.
A POC of the earn module, with a functional Stellar wallet integration on testnet, is already live at
stellar.prismfi.cc/earn, built before requesting any funding. This document describes the architecture of the
full integration, the design decisions behind it, and how each component maps to the deliverables in the Build
submission.
Design principle
Every technical choice in this document was made on a single criterion: efficiency. Minimum new surface
area, maximum reuse of Prism's production infrastructure and of battle-tested Stellar ecosystem components,
shortest verifiable path to mainnet. Where a custom build and an ecosystem building block both solve a
problem, the building block wins: twelve ecosystem integrations, zero custom core infrastructure.
2. Definitions, Acronyms and Abbreviations
 Intent: A declarative cross-chain order settled by a solver network (here, NEAR Intents).
 CCTP V2: Circle's Cross-Chain Transfer Protocol: canonical 1:1 burn-and-mint of native USDC across
supported chains. Live on Stellar mainnet since May 2026.
 Trustline: An explicit opt-in held by a Stellar account to hold a given asset. Each trustline locks a small XLM
reserve.
 Sponsored reserves: A Stellar protocol feature letting one account (Prism) pay the base and trustline
reserves of another account (the user), removing the need for users to hold XLM.
 Fee-bump transaction: A Stellar transaction wrapper letting Prism pay network fees on behalf of a
user-signed transaction.
 Stablebond: A tokenized sovereign bond instrument issued on Stellar by a regulated issuer (Etherfuse,
starting with USTRY) as a standard Stellar asset; an accreting-value token whose redemption value
increases with accrued yield.
 SDEX: Stellar's native on-ledger decentralized exchange, with order books and path payments built into the
protocol.
 DeFindex: SCF-funded vault infrastructure on Stellar; apps plug one API to offer vault strategies routing into
ecosystem DeFi protocols.
 Reflector: Stellar's native oracle network, providing on-chain price and FX feeds.
 Soroswap: Stellar's AMM and DEX aggregator; the Aggregator splits orders across SDEX, Soroswap, and
Aquarius for best execution.
 Blend: An SCF-funded lending protocol on Stellar; idle USDC is supplied to its pool for interim yield.

4. What Is Already Built (POC)
A POC is deployed at stellar.prismfi.cc/earn, built before requesting any funding. It contains two things: a
complete interactive preview of the earn module, and a functional Stellar wallet integration running on testnet.
 Product preview (interface). The full user journey is designed and deployed: the deposit entry point, the
three-step flow (deposit USDC, route to Stellar, allocate to stablebonds), the market selection screen (e.g.,
USTRY as first market, multi-currency expansion), and the position display inside Prism's UI. The product
design is final; what remains is wiring it to live flows.
 Functional Stellar wallet layer (testnet). This part runs today: Stellar Wallets Kit connection, testnet
account creation, on-ledger balance reads, and ownership-proof signing. It is the first Stellar integration code
in the Prism codebase, and it validates the wallet layer described in section 5.1.
The Build Award funds the implementation of the flows behind this interface: the cross-chain deposit routing,
the yield allocation and redemption lifecycle, durable position indexing, and production monitoring, as
specified in section 5. No tranche includes research or system design: the architecture in this document is
final, and part of the integration surface is already running.
5. Integration Design
5.1 Account model and EVM user onboarding
Two user paths are supported. Stellar-native users connect through Stellar Wallets Kit (Creit Tech), the
single library covering all major Stellar wallets, with xBull and Freighter as priority targets. EVM-native users,
the majority of Prism's base, never need to know a Stellar account exists: NEAR Chain Signatures derives a
non-custodial Stellar account controlled by the user's EVM key, so Stellar execution happens behind the
scenes without a new wallet or seed phrase. Prism covers the account's base reserve and asset trustlines

on a production-ready NEAR rail today.
5.2 Bridge layer: cross-chain USDC routing
Deposits route into Stellar through two complementary rails, selected by the backend per source chain and
asset. Circle CCTP V2, live on Stellar mainnet since May 2026, is the canonical route for native USDC from
CCTP domains such as Arbitrum: 1:1 burn-and-mint with no wrapped assets, no solver liquidity caps, and a
hooks mechanism designed for atomic post-mint actions on arrival. NEAR Intents covers chains outside
CCTP coverage (e.g., MegaETH) and non-USDC source assets: the backend requests a quote, the user
signs a single transaction on their EVM wallet, and the solver network settles USDC on the user's Stellar
account, with settlement typically completing in seconds. Solver liquidity caps apply only to the intents route;
the UX caps single transfers there and splits larger amounts into sequential intents. Failure handling on both
routes: transfers are tracked to finality and unconfirmed transfers surface a retry path in the UI. Withdrawal
back to EVM uses the same routing in reverse. The rail is live and launched with strong ecosystem backing;
consumer-facing integrations are only beginning, and reaching the route still takes a multi-step journey.
Prism's contribution is distribution: packaging it into one click and bringing an existing user base, TVL, and
activity onto it.
Efficiency rationale: two production ecosystem rails, zero custom bridge contracts to build, run, or operate. CCTP
V2 is Circle's canonical infrastructure; NEAR Intents extends coverage to 20+ chains and non-USDC assets.
Route selection is backend logic, not new infrastructure.
5.3 RWA yield layer: stablebonds via SDEX and aggregated routing
Yield positions are denominated in Etherfuse stablebonds, tokenized sovereign bond exposure issued on
Stellar as standard Stellar assets. USTRY (US Treasury) is the first market; the structural advantage is the
multi-currency suite behind it (CETES in MXN, TESOURO in BRL, EUROB in EUR), sovereign yield exposure
that is not available to EVM users today and is the core reason Stellar is the rail for this module. Acquisition
runs through two paths selected by order size. Below a configurable threshold, the Stellar Service issues a
PathPaymentStrictReceive directly on the SDEX, Stellar's native on-ledger exchange: atomic, gas-efficient,
no custom contracts. Above the threshold, orders route through the Soroswap Aggregator, which splits
across SDEX, Soroswap AMM pools, and Aquarius stableswap pools for best execution: the Router SDK
simulates split distributions off-chain and the Aggregator contract executes atomically on-chain. The issuer
confirms the deep liquidity venue for these assets is Stellar and that USTRY is purchasable today,
permissionless, with no KYC on the secondary market. A protocol constraint shapes this: SDEX path
payments are Classic operations while Soroswap and Aquarius routes use Soroban (InvokeHostFunction),
and a single Stellar transaction cannot mix the two. Acquisition routes are therefore protocol-homogeneous,

5.4 Strategy layer: custom DeFindex strategy
On top of direct stablebond positions, Prism ships a custom Soroban strategy on DeFindex's vault
framework, aggregating stablebond exposure with automated compounding and plugging into DeFindex's
revenue share framework. DeFindex is SCF-funded vault infrastructure: apps plug one API to offer vault
products, and vaults route into Blend pools with reward swaps via Soroswap (Soroswap and DeFindex are
both built by the same team, PaltaLabs, so the strategy and the routing layer share one integration
relationship). It is the only custom Soroban surface in the project. The core deposit and yield path ships with
zero custom contracts and does not depend on it. The strategy is open-sourced post-launch and hardened
through SCF's standard tranche-3 security process. The team is already in contact with the PaltaLabs team.
Revenue comes from a configurable performance fee on generated yield (a share of the yield, never of
principal), distributed on-chain and split with DeFindex, with the user's displayed APY always shown net. The
fee is set low by design: on consumer sovereign-bond yield, keeping net returns competitive matters more
than fee extraction, so adoption comes first and the fee scales with the value a managed strategy adds.
Oracle risk containment. The V1 strategy is single-asset (USTRY) by design and accounts in bond units:
the share price is bond per share, value accrual lives in the bond itself, and the contract reads no price feed.
No feed, no share-price manipulation vector, and a smaller review surface. Multi-bond aggregation is the V1.x
extension and introduces on-chain FX feeds (Reflector) only with standard hardening: time-averaged prices,
staleness checks, and deviation bounds. In V1, Reflector runs in shadow mode: the backend reads its FX
feeds for display and benchmarks them against external sources, measuring reliability, staleness, and
deviation in production before any contract depends on the feed.
Efficiency rationale: the strategy is a thin contract on a battle-tested, SCF-funded framework, not standalone vault
infrastructure, and its V1 design is oracle-free by construction: less attack surface, less review surface, faster and
safer Tranche 3. One integration inherits the composability stack (DeFindex, Blend, Soroswap) that SDF
documents as the recommended building blocks.
5.5 Idle yield layer: Blend Protocol
Capital a user holds deliberately unallocated, between strategies or parked while deciding, can be put to work
on Blend, an SCF-funded lending protocol on Stellar. This is an opt-in, not an automatic sweep: the fast
deposit path is too short-lived to benefit, so Blend targets pending-allocation balances where the holding
period is long enough to matter, and the user chooses to enable it. Supply and withdraw run through Blend's
public interface (no custom contract) and are instant as long as the pool has liquidity; the integration plugs
into a conservative, well-backstopped pool with a reliable oracle, and gates the opt-in past a high utilization
threshold so a pending allocation is never blocked. Lending carries a residual risk (bad-debt socialization,
standard to all lending markets) which is surfaced to the user at opt-in. Revenue on this layer uses Blend's
Fee Vault in capped-rate mode: the user's return is prioritized up to a target rate and Prism keeps only the
surplus, on-chain and with no custom billing. Blend usage here is distinct from the contract-managed Blend
exposure inside the DeFindex strategy (5.4); the position engine tracks the two separately.

 DeFindex vault framework for the custom strategy layer (Soroban contract on their battle-tested framework).

 Prism's existing React application; the earn module and deposit flow extend the current UI.
 Stellar Wallets Kit for Stellar wallet connectivity (xBull, Freighter priority; Lobstr, Albedo, WalletConnect
supported by the same module).
Development tooling
 Scaffold Stellar as the reference toolkit for any contract work and TypeScript client generation;

 Stellar Lab for endpoint exploration and POC iteration.
Infrastructure and monitoring
 Existing Prism cloud infrastructure; the Stellar service deploys alongside current backend services.
 Tranche 3 adds monitoring and alerting on the Stellar module: bridge settlement latency (alert past 10 min
on CCTP, 5 min on intents); transfer failure rate (circuit breaker past 1% over an hour); trustline and
sponsorship errors (auto-retry with backoff, alert if exhausted); orderbook depth pre-trade check with fallback
venue; Blend pool utilization gate past 95% on the idle-yield opt-in; nightly position reconciliation of indexed
versus on-ledger state.
7. Architecture Constraints
 Non-custodial by design. Prism never takes custody of user funds. Invisible Stellar accounts are derived
through NEAR Chain Signatures and controlled by the user's EVM key; Prism sponsors reserves and fees
but cannot move funds. Smart-account delegation (Protocol 27) is a forward-compatible option as the
standard matures.
 KYC stays in regulated perimeters. Issuer KYC applies to primary issuance and direct redemption with the
issuer; Prism's flow uses the permissionless SDEX secondary market and does not operate a KYC pipeline
for this module.
 Regulated issuance of RWAs. Stablebonds are issued by a regulated issuer; Prism provides interface
access to on-ledger secondary-market liquidity and does not itself issue or distribute securities under
license.
 Jurisdictional gating. RWA yield access is geo-gated per the issuer's distribution coverage; availability
differs by user jurisdiction.
 Inbound flow screening. Cross-chain deposits inherit screening on the settlement path of each route;
flagged flows are rejected before allocation.
